Why qₚ equals ΔH

The equation and what it means

qₚ = ΔH

Use energy conservation and the definition of enthalpy to identify heat flow at constant pressure.

Symbols and units. U: internal energy (J); H: enthalpy (J); qₚ: heat into the system at constant pressure (J); PΔV: pressure–volume energy (J).

Textbook source. Equation 2.18 · Equation 2.19a · Equation 2.19b. Open the exact expression and its context.

What can we start from?

  • First law. ΔU = q + w, with work positive when done on the system.
  • Definition. H = U + PV.
  • Work model. At constant external pressure, PV work is w = −P_ext ΔV.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Insert the work term

Substitute constant-pressure PV work into the first law and solve for q.

ΔU = qₚ − PΔV qₚ = ΔU + PΔV

Some heat changes internal energy; some supplies expansion work.

2. Take the change in enthalpy

Calculate ΔH from its definition. What does constant pressure let you simplify?

ΔH = ΔU + Δ(PV) = ΔU + PΔV

Enthalpy packages internal energy together with the PV term.

3. Compare the expressions

Which two quantities now have identical expressions? Explain the meaning of the equality.

qₚ = ΔH

Under the listed conditions, the enthalpy change equals the heat absorbed. H is a state function; q is still path dependent in general.

Conditions and limits

  • The system is closed and only PV work occurs.
  • Pressure is constant, with initial and final system pressures equal to the imposed external pressure.

Electrical or other non-PV work changes the heat balance. At constant volume with no other work, qᵥ = ΔU instead.

Common misconception

The process must be reversible.

The finite energy balance does not require a reversible path when the stated pressure and work conditions hold.

Check the result

An exothermic reaction in a constant-pressure calorimeter has q_system < 0 and ΔH < 0; the calorimeter gains heat.

At constant pressure, a closed system receives 50 J of heat and does 20 J of PV work. Find ΔU and ΔH.

Work on the system is −20 J, so ΔU = 50 − 20 = 30 J. With only PV work, ΔH = qₚ = 50 J.
