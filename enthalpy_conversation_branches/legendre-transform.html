Why is enthalpy natural at fixed pressure?

Is H = U + PV just a convenient trick?

Start with the fundamental thermodynamic relation

For a simple compressible system of fixed composition, the equilibrium differential of internal energy is dU = T dS − P dV. This combines the reversible definitions of temperature and pressure with the first law.

dH = d(U + PV) = T dS + V dP

Exchange a natural variable

U is naturally expressed as U(S,V); its V derivative is −P. Adding PV cancels the dV term and replaces volume with pressure as an independent variable. This operation is a Legendre transform, familiar from switching variables in analytical mechanics.

T = (∂H/∂S)P, V = (∂H/∂P)S

The transform does not add an energy source. It changes which controlled variables appear directly in the differential.

Connect to heat capacity

At fixed P and fixed composition, the heat needed for an infinitesimal reversible temperature increase is dH. Consequently Cₚ = (∂H/∂T)P. By contrast, holding V fixed makes dU the corresponding heat, giving Cᵥ = (∂U/∂T)V.

Neither derivative requires heat capacity to be independent of temperature.
