What does enthalpy add to internal energy?

Why introduce U + PV?

Make room in the surroundings

An expanding closed system must displace its surroundings. At a fixed imposed pressure P, creating an extra volume ΔV transfers PΔV of energy outward as mechanical work. Heat supplied to the system must cover both this transfer and the internal-energy increase.

q = ΔU + PΔV

Package the pressure constraint into a state function

Define H = U + PV. At constant pressure, its finite change is exactly the combination in the heat balance. H remains defined for every equilibrium state, whether or not the actual experiment holds pressure constant.

dH = dU + P dV + V dP dH = δq + V dP (reversible PV work only)

Enthalpy is not heat stored inside matter. Heat describes energy crossing a boundary; enthalpy assigns a value to an equilibrium state. Their changes coincide only for specified constraints.

Closed-system heat versus flow energy

The PV term also appears in a steady-flow apparatus because a stream must be pushed into or out of a control volume. That is a separate energy balance. The present proof concerns a closed system, so no mass carries energy across its boundary.

For a constant-pressure process absorbing 50 J while doing 20 J of expansion work, ΔU = 30 J and ΔH = 50 J.
