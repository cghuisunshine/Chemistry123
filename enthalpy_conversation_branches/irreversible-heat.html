Does q at constant pressure require reversibility?

Can an irreversible reaction still have qₚ = ΔH?

Use a finite balance rather than a reversible differential

Assume a closed system expands against a constant Pext, only PV work occurs, piston energy storage is negligible, and initial and final system pressures equal Pext. The intermediate states may be irreversible.

ΔU = q − Pext(V₂ − V₁) ΔH = ΔU + (P₂V₂ − P₁V₁) P₁ = P₂ = Pext ⇒ ΔH = q

The endpoint condition does real work

During a rapid reaction the gas need not possess a uniform equilibrium pressure. We do not insert such an intermediate pressure into the work integral. The constant external load determines the work; the equilibrium endpoints determine Δ(PV). Their pressure values match in the stated experiment.

What changes with electrical work?

ΔU = q − PΔV + wnon-PV,on ΔH = q + wnon-PV,on

A cell delivering electrical work has wnon-PV,on negative. Its heat transfer is therefore not determined by ΔH alone. Constant pressure by itself is insufficient; the work terms must also be specified.
