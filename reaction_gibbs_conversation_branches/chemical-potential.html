What is chemical potential, and why a logarithm?

Where does μ = μ° + RT ln a come from?

An energy cost per added mole

Chemical potential is the partial molar Gibbs energy: μᵢ = (∂G/∂nᵢ)T,P,nⱼ. It measures how G changes when a little of species i is added while the other amounts, temperature, and pressure are held fixed. The mixture volume can change in this definition.

Derive the pressure dependence for an ideal gas

For a pure fluid, dμ = −sₘ dT + vₘ dP. On an ideal-gas isotherm vₘ = RT/P. Integrate from a standard pressure p° to P:

μ(T,P) − μ°(T) = ∫p°P RT dP′/P′ = RT ln(P/p°)

The logarithm is therefore a consequence of the ideal-gas volume response. In an ideal mixture the appropriate pressure is the species partial pressure pᵢ.

Generalize using activity

aᵢ ≡ exp[(μᵢ − μᵢ°)/(RT)]

For a real mixture this equation defines thermodynamic activity relative to its standard state. Ideal gases have aᵢ = pᵢ/p°; real gases use fugacity ratios. A dilute solute is often modeled by aᵢ = γᵢcᵢ/c°. The logarithmic activity form is general, but these concentration and pressure substitutions are models.
