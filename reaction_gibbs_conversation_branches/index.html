Reaction Gibbs energy and Q

Show why chemical composition enters reaction Gibbs energy through a logarithm of the reaction quotient.

The equation and what it means

ΔᵣG = ΔᵣG° + RT ln Q Q = ∏ aᵢνᵢ

Show why chemical composition enters reaction Gibbs energy through a logarithm of the reaction quotient.

Symbols and units. μᵢ: J mol⁻¹; νᵢ: signed stoichiometric coefficient; ξ: mol of reaction; ΔᵣG: J mol⁻¹ of reaction; aᵢ and Q: dimensionless.

What can we start from?

  • Fundamental. dG = −S dT + V dP + Σ μᵢ dnᵢ; at fixed T,P, μᵢ is the partial molar Gibbs energy.
  • Definition. For extent ξ, dnᵢ = νᵢ dξ, with νᵢ positive for products and negative for reactants.
  • Activity. μᵢ = μᵢ° + RT ln aᵢ defines activity relative to a chosen standard state. Ideal gas: aᵢ = pᵢ/p°.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Introduce reaction extent

Substitute dnᵢ = νᵢ dξ into dG at fixed T and P, then divide by dξ.

ΔᵣG = Σ νᵢ μᵢ

Stoichiometry weights the chemical potential contribution of each species.

2. Insert the activity relation

Replace each μᵢ and separate the standard and composition terms.

ΔᵣG = Σ νᵢ μᵢ° + RT Σ νᵢ ln aᵢ

At a common temperature, RT factors out of the composition sum.

↳ Follow-up: Where does μ = μ° + RT ln a come from? What is chemical potential, and why a logarithm?Follow the argument, then return to this exact statement. ↗

3. Combine the logarithms

Use logarithm rules to turn the weighted sum into a product.

Σ νᵢ ln aᵢ = ln(∏ aᵢνᵢ) = ln Q

Negative reactant coefficients put their activities in the denominator.

4. Name the standard reaction change

Define ΔᵣG° and write the final result. For A ⇌ 2B, write Q.

ΔᵣG = ΔᵣG° + RT ln Q For A ⇌ 2B: Q = a_B²/a_A

Q changes as composition changes; the standard reaction Gibbs energy at fixed T is the reference value.

Conditions and limits

  • Temperature and pressure are fixed when defining the reaction derivative.
  • Q uses dimensionless activities and the same standard-state convention as ΔᵣG°.

Here ΔᵣG is (∂G/∂ξ) at the current composition, not the total finite change in G over an entire reaction. The activity relation is an explicit thermodynamic starting point.

Common misconception

The mixture must already be at equilibrium.

This relation describes a reaction mixture at any composition. At equilibrium a further condition, ΔᵣG = 0, applies.

Check the result

For ideal gases use pᵢ/p°. For dilute solutions an approximation is aᵢ ≈ cᵢ/c°. Pure condensed phases have activity approximately 1 in their usual standard states.

For A ⇌ 2B, if a_A = 0.50 and a_B = 0.20, what is Q?

Q = a_B²/a_A = 0.20²/0.50 = 0.080. The product coefficient becomes an exponent.
