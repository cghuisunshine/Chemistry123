Entropy change of an ideal gas

Derive the entropy change between two ideal-gas states and see why the actual path need not be reversible.

The equation and what it means

ΔS = nCᵥ,ₘ ln(T₂/T₁) + nR ln(V₂/V₁)

Derive the entropy change between two ideal-gas states and see why the actual path need not be reversible.

Symbols and units. S: entropy (J K⁻¹); Cᵥ,ₘ: molar heat capacity (J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹); T: K; both ratios inside ln are dimensionless.

What can we start from?

  • Definition. dS = δq_rev/T along a reversible path between equilibrium states.
  • First law. dU = δq_rev − P dV for a reversible path with only PV work.
  • Model. Ideal gas: P = nRT/V and dU = nCᵥ,ₘ dT at fixed composition.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Solve for reversible heat

Rearrange the first law to express δq_rev in terms of dU and dV.

δqrev = dU + P dV

This is an energy balance along the reversible calculation path.

2. Use the entropy definition

Divide by T, then substitute the ideal-gas expressions.

dS = nCᵥ,ₘ dT/T + nR dV/V

Temperature and volume contributions separate for an ideal gas.

3. Integrate between endpoint states

Integrate both terms with constant Cᵥ,ₘ.

ΔS = nCᵥ,ₘ ln(T₂/T₁) + nR ln(V₂/V₁)

Entropy depends on the endpoints, so this value also applies to an irreversible process between those same states.

↳ Follow-up: Which part of the logarithmic formula is approximate? What if heat capacity is not constant?Follow the argument, then return to this exact statement. ↗

4. Test a limiting case

What remains for an isothermal expansion? What is the sign?

ΔSisothermal = nR ln(V₂/V₁) > 0 for V₂ > V₁

An isolated ideal gas can gain entropy in free expansion even though q_actual = 0.

Conditions and limits

  • The gas is ideal and its amount and composition are fixed.
  • Cᵥ,ₘ is constant over the temperature interval for the logarithmic temperature term.

If Cᵥ,ₘ depends on temperature, retain n∫[T₁→T₂] Cᵥ,ₘ(T) dT/T. Never replace δq_rev by actual irreversible heat in the definition.

Common misconception

The actual process must be reversible.

Entropy is a state function; evaluate it on an imagined reversible path connecting the same equilibrium endpoints.

Check the result

For 1.00 mol doubling its volume at fixed temperature, ΔS = 8.314 ln 2 = 5.76 J K⁻¹.

An ideal gas freely expands into vacuum in an insulated rigid enclosure. Its equilibrium volume doubles. What is ΔS of the gas?

No heat or work crosses the gas boundary, so ΔU = 0 and its temperature is unchanged. A reversible isothermal calculation path gives ΔS = nR ln 2.
