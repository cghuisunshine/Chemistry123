Can state counting explain the volume term?

Why does more room mean nR ln(V₂/V₁)?

Count accessible positions

For N noninteracting classical particles at fixed total energy, each particle position can range over volume V. The position part of the phase-space volume is therefore proportional to VN. Momentum factors are unchanged when comparing equal-energy ideal-gas states.

Ω₂/Ω₁ = (V₂/V₁)N

Apply Boltzmann entropy

ΔS = kB ln(Ω₂/Ω₁) = NkB ln(V₂/V₁) = nR ln(V₂/V₁)

The phase-space normalization, the indistinguishability factor N!, and the momentum contribution cancel in this fixed-N ratio. This calculation explains the isothermal volume term; it is not a derivation of the full temperature term for every type of molecule.

Why free expansion increases entropy

Removing a partition opens additional accessible positions without changing ideal-gas internal energy. The equilibrium macrostate occupies vastly more microstates. There is no contradiction with energy conservation: energy constrains which states are accessible, while entropy measures their statistical multiplicity.

For 1.00 mol doubling its volume, the change is R ln 2 = 5.76 J K⁻¹ whether the expansion is reversible or free.
