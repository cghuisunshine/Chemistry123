What if heat capacity is not constant?

Which part of the logarithmic formula is approximate?

Keep the temperature integral

ΔS = n∫T₁T₂ Cᵥ,ₘ(T) dT/T + nR ln(V₂/V₁)

The volume term remains logarithmic for an ideal gas of fixed composition. Only the temperature integral required constant Cᵥ,ₘ in the short formula. Molecular vibrational excitation can make that assumption inaccurate over a large interval.

Integrate a simple temperature-dependent model

Suppose Cᵥ,ₘ(T) = a + bT over the relevant range, where a has units J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and b has units J mol⁻¹ K⁻². Then:

ΔS = n[a ln(T₂/T₁) + b(T₂ − T₁)] + nR ln(V₂/V₁)

Every term has units J K⁻¹. The expression reduces continuously to the constant-heat-capacity formula as b tends to zero.

An alternative using pressure

Use V₂/V₁ = (T₂/T₁)(P₁/P₂) and Cₚ,ₘ(T) = Cᵥ,ₘ(T) + R. The same endpoint entropy change can be written:

ΔS = n∫T₁T₂ Cₚ,ₘ(T) dT/T − nR ln(P₂/P₁)

These equivalent forms are a useful check on both algebra and sign.
