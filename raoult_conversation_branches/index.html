Raoult’s law

Connect solvent mole fraction to equilibrium partial vapour pressure for an ideal liquid solution.

The equation and what it means

pA = xA pA*

Connect solvent mole fraction to equilibrium partial vapour pressure for an ideal liquid solution.

Symbols and units. xA: dimensionless mole fraction of A in the liquid; pA: its partial vapour pressure; pA*: pure-A saturation vapour pressure at the same T, in matching units.

Textbook source. Equation 5.24. Open the exact expression and its context.

What can we start from?

  • Equilibrium. The chemical potential of A is equal in liquid and vapour.
  • Ideal solution. μA(l) = μA*(l) + RT ln xA.
  • Ideal vapour. At the same T, μA(g,pA) − μA(g,pA*) = RT ln(pA/pA*); pure A has μA*(l) = μA(g,pA*).
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Subtract the pure-liquid reference

Write the change in liquid chemical potential on mixing.

μA(l) − μA*(l) = RT ln xA

Mixing lowers the chemical potential when 0 < xA < 1.

2. Apply liquid–vapour equilibrium

Replace each liquid chemical potential by its equilibrium vapour counterpart.

RT ln xA = RT ln(pA/pA*)

The same lowering is reflected by reduced equilibrium partial pressure.

3. Exponentiate and interpret

Cancel RT and take the exponential.

pA = xA pA*

At xA = 1 the pure-component pressure is recovered; dilution lowers A’s partial pressure.

Conditions and limits

  • The liquid follows the ideal-solution chemical-potential model for A.
  • The vapour is approximately ideal; liquid pressure corrections are negligible.

For a real solution use an appropriate activity correction, pA ≈ aA pA* in the stated vapour approximation. Henry’s law uses a different reference for a dilute solute.

Common misconception

The vapour mole fraction equals the liquid mole fraction.

Raoult’s law uses the liquid mole fraction xA. The vapour fraction is yA = pA/p and can be very different.

Check the result

For an ideal binary liquid, p = xA pA* + (1 − xA)pB*. The vapour fraction is then yA = xA pA*/p.

An ideal solution has xA = 0.40 and pA* = 50 kPa. What is pA?

pA = 0.40 × 50 kPa = 20 kPa. This is a component partial pressure, not necessarily the total pressure.
