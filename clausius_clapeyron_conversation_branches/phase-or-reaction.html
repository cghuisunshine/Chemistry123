Why does this resemble the van ’t Hoff equation?

Does the same-looking formula describe the same quantity?

Recognize their common thermodynamics

phase change: d ln p/dT = ΔvapHₘ/(RT²) chemical reaction: d ln K/dT = ΔᵣH°/(RT²)

Both connect equilibrium to a change in enthalpy. But p is a coexistence vapour pressure, whereas K is a dimensionless activity-based reaction quotient at equilibrium. Their standard states and physical approximations differ.

Check the sign with a numerical ratio

Using 40.0 kJ mol⁻¹, T₁ = 300 K and T₂ = 310 K gives ln(p₂/p₁) = 0.517 and p₂/p₁ = 1.68. A positive latent heat means the equilibrium vapour pressure rises on heating. Taking logarithms of the ratio also avoids any dependence on whether both pressures were reported in Pa or kPa.
