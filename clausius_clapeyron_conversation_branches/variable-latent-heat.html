What if vaporization enthalpy changes with temperature?

Which step actually needs a constant latent heat?

Retain the integral

ln(p₂/p₁) = (1/R)∫T₁T₂ ΔvapHₘ(T) dT/T²

The differential equation already allows temperature-dependent enthalpy. Pulling ΔHₘ outside this integral is the extra approximation behind the familiar two-temperature expression.

A simple correction model

Suppose along the considered coexistence interval ΔvapHₘ(T) is adequately approximated by H₁ + b(T − T₁), where b has units J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Integration gives:

ln(p₂/p₁) = [(H₁ − bT₁)(1/T₁ − 1/T₂) + b ln(T₂/T₁)]/R

The expression reduces to the constant-enthalpy result when b = 0. Treat b as a specified fit over the interval; pressure-dependent enthalpy corrections require additional care.
