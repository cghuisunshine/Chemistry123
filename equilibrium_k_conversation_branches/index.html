Why ΔᵣG° = −RT ln K

Connect the equilibrium composition to standard reaction Gibbs energy without confusing ΔᵣG with ΔᵣG°.

The equation and what it means

ΔᵣG° = −RT ln K K = exp(−ΔᵣG°/RT)

Connect the equilibrium composition to standard reaction Gibbs energy without confusing ΔᵣG with ΔᵣG°.

Symbols and units. ΔᵣG°: J mol⁻¹ of reaction; R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; T: K; K: dimensionless thermodynamic equilibrium constant.

Textbook source. Equation 7.16 · Equation 7.17. Open the exact expression and its context.

What can we start from?

  • Thermodynamics. ΔᵣG = ΔᵣG° + RT ln Q, from chemical potentials and reaction stoichiometry.
  • Equilibrium. At fixed T,P, an interior chemical equilibrium has ΔᵣG = 0.
  • Definition. K is the activity-based reaction quotient evaluated at equilibrium.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Apply equilibrium to the general relation

Insert both equilibrium conditions: one for ΔᵣG and one for Q.

0 = ΔᵣG° + RT ln K

Equilibrium composition balances the standard and composition contributions.

2. Isolate the standard Gibbs energy

Rearrange the expression; check its sign if K > 1.

ΔᵣG° = −RT ln K

If K > 1 then ln K > 0, so ΔᵣG° < 0. That does not mean the equilibrium mixture is still reacting forward spontaneously.

3. Solve for K

Divide by −RT and exponentiate.

K = exp(−ΔᵣG°/RT)

A modest standard energy difference can produce a large equilibrium ratio because of the exponential.

Conditions and limits

  • The reaction is at chemical equilibrium at the specified temperature.
  • K uses dimensionless activities consistent with the standard Gibbs energy.

K depends on the reaction as written. Reversing the reaction changes K to 1/K and changes the sign of ΔᵣG°.

Common misconception

ΔᵣG° must equal zero at equilibrium.

The actual reaction Gibbs energy is zero. Its standard-state value is zero only if K = 1.

Check the result

At 298 K and K = 10, ΔᵣG° = −8.314 × 298 × ln 10 = −5.70 kJ mol⁻¹.

At equilibrium, K = 100. Which statement is correct?

The actual reaction Gibbs energy is zero at equilibrium. Since ln 100 > 0, ΔᵣG° = −RT ln 100 is negative.
