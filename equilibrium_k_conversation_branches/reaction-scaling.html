Why does K depend on how the reaction is written?

What happens if I reverse or double the equation?

Reverse the stoichiometric coefficients

Replacing every νᵢ by −νᵢ reverses the reaction. The activity quotient becomes its reciprocal, and the standard reaction Gibbs energy changes sign.

Qreverse = 1/Q ΔᵣG°reverse = −ΔᵣG° Kreverse = 1/K

Multiply the written reaction by a factor

If every coefficient is multiplied by a positive number λ, ΔᵣG° is multiplied by λ and Q becomes Qλ. Applying the exponential equilibrium relation gives:

Kscaled = Kλ

For A ⇌ B with K = 10, writing 2A ⇌ 2B gives K = 100. The physical equilibrium composition is the same because its quotient was also squared.

Add two independent reaction equations

Standard reaction Gibbs energies add when the reactions are added stoichiometrically. Exponentiating their sum makes equilibrium constants multiply.

ΔᵣG°sum = ΔᵣG°₁ + ΔᵣG°₂ Ksum = K₁K₂

All constants must use the same temperature and compatible standard-state conventions.
