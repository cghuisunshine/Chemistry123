Why must K be dimensionless?

What does the standard-state symbol mean?

A logarithm compares like quantities

The argument of a logarithm cannot carry a physical unit. Chemical potentials use activities such as pᵢ/p° or γᵢcᵢ/c°, which compare a pressure or concentration to a reference with the same units.

Q = ∏ aᵢνᵢ, K = Qeq

Raw concentration products need a convention

For A ⇌ 2B in an ideal dilute solution, the activity quotient is ([B]/c°)²/([A]/c°) = [B]²/([A]c°). Omitting c° leaves a quantity with concentration units. Its numerical value changes if concentrations are expressed in different units.

The thermodynamic K is invariant to a mere unit conversion because c° is converted too. A change of the physical standard-state convention must instead be accompanied by a consistent change in μ° and ΔᵣG°.

Standard does not mean equilibrium

The superscript ° denotes reference states, not a mixture that is necessarily at equilibrium. ΔᵣG° is the stoichiometric sum of reference chemical potentials. ΔᵣG = 0 describes the actual equilibrium mixture; ΔᵣG° = 0 occurs only when the corresponding thermodynamic K is 1.
