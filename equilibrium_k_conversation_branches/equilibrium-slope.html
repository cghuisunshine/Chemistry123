Why is ΔᵣG zero at equilibrium?

Why set the actual reaction driving force to zero?

A minimum under material balance

At fixed T and P with no externally imposed useful work, a closed reacting mixture relaxes toward a constrained minimum of G. Use the reaction extent ξ to satisfy material balance automatically.

dG = ΔᵣG dξ

Allow both directions of a small reaction

At an interior equilibrium a little forward reaction and a little reverse reaction are both feasible. If ΔᵣG were negative, increasing ξ would lower G. If it were positive, decreasing ξ would lower G. Only a zero first derivative can be stationary.

ΔᵣGeq = 0 0 = ΔᵣG° + RT ln Qeq

Stability additionally requires the stationary state to be a minimum. At a boundary of feasible composition, use the allowed-direction test instead.

Use K to test another composition

ΔᵣG = RT ln(Q/K)

For Q < K the forward reaction lowers G; for Q > K the reverse reaction lowers G. At Q = K there is no net thermodynamic driving force. Molecular forward and reverse events can continue at equal rates.
