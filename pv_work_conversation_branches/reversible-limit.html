What does the reversible limit mean mechanically?

How can a piston move if the pressures are equal?

Keep an infinitesimal imbalance

For expansion, choose Pext = P − ε with ε positive and arbitrarily small, and remove tiny weights as the gas expands. A literal exact balance would produce no acceleration from rest. Reversibility describes the limiting sequence of states as the required imbalance tends to zero.

δw = −(P − ε) dV → −P dV as ε → 0

Slow is necessary but not sufficient

A piston sliding slowly with friction still dissipates energy. Reversing its motion requires overcoming friction in the opposite direction, so the original path is not recovered by an infinitesimal change of load. A reversible model also excludes friction and finite temperature gradients during heat exchange.

Why this limit gives the largest output

Along an isothermal ideal-gas expansion, a load greater than the gas pressure would stop outward motion. A smaller load receives less work per volume increment. A load infinitesimally below the gas pressure therefore gives the largest work output among these expansion paths.

work output = −w; wrev is most negative

This statement compares expansion between the specified endpoints with the specified thermal constraints; it is not a rule for unrelated paths.
