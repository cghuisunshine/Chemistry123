Why is work a path quantity?

Can the same endpoints give different work?

Use identical ideal-gas endpoints

Let a fixed amount of ideal gas begin at T and V₁ and finish at the same T and V₂ = 2V₁. One possible route is a reversible isothermal expansion maintained by a heat bath. Another is insulated free expansion into vacuum, followed by equilibration.

wrev = −nRT ln 2 wfree = 0

For the free expansion of an ideal gas, ΔU = 0 implies the final temperature is unchanged. Both routes therefore connect the specified states, but exchange different amounts of work.

Energy conservation still holds

ΔU = q + w = 0 qrev = nRT ln 2, qfree = 0

The state function is internal energy. Heat and work can trade off while their sum remains fixed by the endpoints. On a quasistatic pressure–volume diagram, −w is the signed area under the path; endpoint coordinates do not specify that area.

A cycle exposes the distinction

Return to the initial state along a different route. Every state function has zero net change over the cycle. Work need not vanish, because the forward and return paths can enclose an area. The cycle then exchanges an equal and opposite net amount of heat.
