Pressure–volume work

Derive the work done on a gas when a piston moves against an external pressure.

The equation and what it means

w = −∫V₁V₂ Pext dV Constant Pext: w = −Pext(V₂ − V₁)

Derive the work done on a gas when a piston moves against an external pressure.

Symbols and units. w: work on the system (J); P_ext: external pressure (Pa); V: volume (m³). 1 Pa·m³ = 1 J. Heat and work are positive when energy enters the system.

What can we start from?

  • Definition. Work on a system is force dotted with the displacement of its boundary.
  • Definition. Pressure is normal force per area: P = F/A.
  • Geometry. For a piston of area A, a displacement dx changes volume by dV = A dx.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Fix the sign

For an outward piston displacement dx > 0, what direction does the external force point? Write the infinitesimal work on the gas.

δw = −Fext dx

Expansion transfers energy from the gas to its surroundings, so the work on the gas is negative.

2. Replace force and distance

Use pressure and piston geometry to eliminate F_ext and dx.

δw = −Pext A dx = −Pext dV

The piston area cancels. This is why pressure and volume alone describe this boundary work.

3. Add up the small changes

Write the general integral, then simplify only when external pressure is constant.

w = −∫V₁V₂ Pext dV w = −Pext(V₂ − V₁) if Pext is constant

The integral adds the work over the actual path. Work is a path quantity, not a state function.

Conditions and limits

  • Only pressure–volume work is included.
  • The external pressure is constant for the simplified finite formula.

Use external pressure for actual boundary work. Substitute the gas pressure only along a mechanically reversible path.

Common misconception

The gas must be ideal.

The boundary-work argument does not need an ideal-gas equation of state.

Check the result

At zero external pressure (free expansion), PV work is zero even if the volume increases.

A gas expands by 2.0 L against a constant 100 kPa. What is the work on the gas?

2.0 L = 0.0020 m³, so w = −100000 × 0.0020 = −200 J. Expansion makes w negative.
