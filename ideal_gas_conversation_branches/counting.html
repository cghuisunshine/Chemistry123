Why do microstate counts give probabilities?

The counting argument behind the reservoir-probability rule.

Fix one particular molecular microstate

Take a small system S (our molecule) and a large reservoir R. A complete microstate is a pair (s, r): one molecule state plus one reservoir state.

If the molecule is in state s, with energy εs, the reservoir’s energy must be Etotal − εs. There are exactly ΩR(Etotal − εs) allowed reservoir states that can pair with this one molecular state.

Where does the exponential come from?

The counting rule involves Ω, a number of microstates. In the earlier temperature derivation we replaced that count by the entropy expression Ω = eS/kB, and then obtained e−ε/(kBT).

Where the proof stops

Equal a priori probabilities are an equilibrium postulate, not a direct mathematical consequence of Newton’s laws. Hamiltonian mechanics offers supporting structure, but does not alone prove that every initial distribution becomes uniform.
