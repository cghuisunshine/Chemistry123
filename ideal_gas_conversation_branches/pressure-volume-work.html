Why is pressure–volume work −p dV?

Track a piston displacement and the direction of the external load before taking the reversible limit.

1. Relate piston motion to volume change

Imagine a cylinder containing gas on the left of a frictionless piston of area A. Take x to increase outward to the right. Moving the piston through dx changes the gas volume by

dV = A dx

2. Calculate work using the external force

Pressure is force per area. The external load acts inward, so its force in the chosen x-direction is −pextA. With a frictionless piston and negligible piston kinetic-energy storage, the work on the gas is

Fext,x = −pextA δWon = Fext,x dx = −pextA dx = −pext dV

During expansion, force and displacement have opposite signs, so the external load does negative work on the gas. Equivalently, the gas does positive work on the surroundings.

3. Take the reversible mechanical limit

For reversible expansion, the external load is infinitesimally below the equilibrium gas pressure. For reversible compression, it is infinitesimally above. In that limit,

pext → p δWon,rev = −p dV

The limiting sequence retains arbitrarily small driving imbalances. A literal exact force balance would not start a stationary piston moving. A finite pressure imbalance instead requires retaining the external pressure in the work balance.

4. Check the sign and units

Suppose a gas expands by 1.0 L against a constant external pressure of 100 kPa:

Won = −pext ΔV = −(100 kPa)(1.0 L) = −100 J

Here 1 kPa·L = 1 J. Compression through the same volume magnitude gives +100 J of work on the gas. For a changing load, integrate −pext dV along the specified path.

5. Separate work transfers from state changes

δW marks a path-dependent transfer. There is no general state function W whose difference equals the work along every path. The expression −p dV is the reversible volume-work element; it is not the differential of −pV, which also contains −V dp.

Can gas expansion into vacuum do pressure–volume work on an external load?

With p_ext = 0, the external pressure–volume work is zero. A nonzero pressure inside the gas does not by itself mean that an external load receives energy.
