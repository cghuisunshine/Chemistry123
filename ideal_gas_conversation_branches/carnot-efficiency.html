Why do reversible engines have the same efficiency?

Combine two complete cycles and track every heat and work transfer.

1. State the second-law premise

Use the Kelvin–Planck form of the second law: no cyclic device can have as its sole effect the extraction of heat from one equilibrium thermal reservoir and the delivery of positive work. With every auxiliary restored except an ideal mechanical work store, this setup requires

Wout ≤ 0 (cyclic device; one heat reservoir; no other net changes)
An alternative second-law starting point

If entropy is already established, a cyclic engine, one heat reservoir, and ideal work storage would have total entropy change −Q/T. Positive heat extraction would contradict ΔStotal ≥ 0.

This is a physical premise, not a consequence of energy conservation alone. If entropy and its second-law inequality have already been established, the same prohibition follows from an entropy balance. The linked explanation gives that alternative proof and states its assumptions.

2. Assume unequal efficiencies and match the heat amounts

Suppose reversible engines A and B operate between the same hot and cold reservoirs, with 0 < ηB < ηA < 1. Scale the engine sizes or cycle amounts so that each forward engine absorbs the same Q > 0 from the hot reservoir. Equal heat amounts do not require identical engine sizes.

η ≡ Wout/Q WA = ηAQ Qc,A = Q − WA = (1 − ηA)Q WB = ηBQ Qc,B = (1 − ηB)Q

Qc,A and Qc,B are positive magnitudes of heat rejected to the cold reservoir by the forward engines. Heat and work have units J; efficiency is dimensionless. These equalities use the first law over a full cycle, ΔU = 0.

3. Reverse B and add the signed transfers

Run A forward and B backward. Reversibility permits every heat and work transfer of B to reverse exactly. Count heat into the combined device and work delivered by it as positive:

Signed transfers for complete cycles; Q > 0.
TransferA forwardB backwardCombined
Heat from hot reservoirQ−Q0
Heat from cold reservoir−(1−ηA)Q+(1−ηB)Q(ηA−ηB)Q
Work deliveredηAQ−ηBQ(ηA−ηB)Q
Qh,net = Q − Q = 0 Qc,net = −(1 − ηA)Q + (1 − ηB)Q = (ηA − ηB)Q > 0 Wnet = (ηA − ηB)Q > 0 ΔUA = ΔUB = 0

The hot reservoir has no net change. Both devices return to their original states. The sole net effect is to take Qc,net from the cold reservoir and deliver the identical amount as work. The cold reservoir is the only reservoir with a net energy change. Energy is conserved, but this is precisely what Kelvin–Planck forbids.

4. Show the contradiction and check with numbers

Second law for this setup: Wnet ≤ 0 Assumed unequal efficiencies: Wnet > 0 Therefore ηA > ηB is impossible.

If instead ηB > ηA, swap the engines and repeat the same construction. Both strict inequalities are impossible, so

ηA,rev = ηB,rev

For a numerical check, suppose ηA = 0.40, ηB = 0.30, and Q = 1000 J. A produces 400 J and rejects 600 J. Reversed B consumes 300 J, extracts 700 J from the cold reservoir, and returns 1000 J to the hot reservoir. The combined device takes 100 J from the cold reservoir and produces 100 J of work. These assumed efficiencies are a contradiction example, not two physically realizable reversible engines at the same temperatures.

Does this prove all engines have the same efficiency?

No. Equality is established for reversible engines between the same reservoirs. An irreversible engine need not run backward with exactly reversed transfers. The related comparison shows that no engine can outperform a reversible engine between those reservoirs.
