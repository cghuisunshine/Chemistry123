Why does the exponential appear?

Unpack the logarithm, temperature derivative, and Boltzmann factor.

1. Why can entropy replace a state count?

Boltzmann's entropy relation states:

Divide both sides by kB and exponentiate. This is just algebra:

S / kB = ln Ω   ⟹   Ω = eS/kB

So replacing Ω with eS/kB does not add another physical assumption beyond the entropy relation itself.

2. Why a logarithm in the first place?

For independent systems A and B, each state of A can be paired with every state of B. Their state counts therefore multiply:

But thermodynamic entropies of independent systems add:

A continuous, increasing function that turns products into sums is a logarithm. That motivates S = C ln Ω; identifying its thermodynamic scale gives C = kB.

Why this is not circular

Counting motivates the logarithm because it makes entropy extensive. Matching the statistical definition to thermodynamic temperature fixes the constant and links the microscopic and macroscopic descriptions.

3. Why does the derivative equal 1/T?

The thermodynamic definition of entropy for reversible heat transfer is dS = δQrev/T. At fixed volume and particle number, where only pressure–volume work is considered, the first law gives δQrev = dE.

It also explains thermal equilibrium: energy tends to be distributed so that total entropy is maximal. At its maximum, the two subsystems have equal ∂S/∂E, hence equal temperature.

4. Why does an exponential probability appear?

Let a molecule take energy ε from a huge thermal reservoir. The reservoir's energy decreases by ε, so its entropy decreases approximately by ε/T.

We now convert entropy back into a count. The constant part of the exponent only changes the overall normalization:

Because a molecular microstate’s probability is proportional to the number of compatible reservoir states:

Physical meaning: at positive temperature, a molecule with more energy leaves less energy to the reservoir. The reservoir generally has fewer compatible states, so that particular high-energy molecular microstate is less probable.

What has been established?

We have traced the exponential weight back through (i) compatible-state counting, (ii) the logarithmic entropy relation, and (iii) the thermodynamic temperature derivative. The algebra is exact within the large-reservoir approximation, while the physical framework still relies on equilibrium statistical mechanics.
