Why does the second law forbid a single-reservoir cyclic engine?

Add the entropy changes of the engine, reservoir, and work storage.

1. Start with the entropy form of the second law

Here assume entropy is already a state function and the entropy form of the second law is established. For an isolated total system,

ΔStotal ≥ 0

Test a proposed engine that completes a cycle, absorbs Q > 0 from one ideal equilibrium reservoir at constant positive temperature T, and turns all that heat into work. Its only other effect is to raise an ideal weight. Enclose the engine, reservoir, and weight together as the isolated total system. There is no fuel consumption, second heat bath, depletion of a prepared device state, or other compensating change.

ΔUengine = 0 Q − Wout = 0 Wout = Q > 0

The first law permits this energy balance. We must now check the entropy balance. Q and W are measured in joules, T in kelvin, and entropy in J/K.

2. Calculate each entropy change

Entropy changes for the proposed complete cycle.
PartEntropy changeReason
EngineΔSengine = 0A full cycle restores its state; entropy is a state function.
Thermal reservoirΔSreservoir = −Q/TIt loses heat Q at constant equilibrium temperature T.
Ideal work storageΔSweight = 0Raising a weight changes gravitational potential energy without changing its internal thermodynamic state.

The reservoir result refers to its own equilibrium states. It can be evaluated using a reversible comparison, even if the engine is irreversible. The engine itself need not be reversible: its net entropy change is zero because it completes a cycle. The ideal weight stores mechanical energy without an entropy change; frictional heating or any other change would have to be included as an additional effect.

Why can raising an ideal weight leave its entropy unchanged?

Take a rigid weight raised gently from rest at height h₁ to rest at h₂, without heating, friction, or a change of deformation. Its internal thermodynamic state is unchanged. Only its gravitational potential energy changes:

ΔEweight = mg(h₂ − h₁) > 0 ΔUinternal = 0 ΔSweight = 0

A 1 kg weight lifted by 1 m stores about 9.8 J. Its molecules need not become hotter or acquire more accessible internal states. Energy stored in the position of the whole body is different from energy dissipated into its molecular motion.

A microscopic check makes this precise. In the ideal model, fixing the whole weight at height h adds the same mgh to every internal energy level εᵢ. At unchanged temperature T, let β = 1/(kBT):

Eᵢ(h) = εᵢ + mgh Z(h) = ∑ᵢ exp[−β(εᵢ + mgh)] = exp(−βmgh) Zinternal pᵢ(h) = exp[−β(εᵢ + mgh)] / Z(h) = exp(−βεᵢ) / Zinternal S = −kB ∑ᵢ pᵢ ln pᵢ

The common gravitational factor cancels from every probability, so the internal entropy is unchanged. This assumes the weight’s macroscopic position is controlled and its internal level spacings are unchanged. Work can generate entropy when it is dissipated through friction or other irreversible processes; zero entropy change here is a property of ideal mechanical storage, not a rule for every object receiving work.

3. Add the changes and obtain the contradiction

ΔStotal = ΔSengine + ΔSreservoir + ΔSweight = 0 − Q/T + 0 = −Q/T < 0 (Q > 0, T > 0)

The proposed device would reduce the entropy of the isolated total, contradicting ΔStotal ≥ 0. Equivalently, the second law requires

−Q/T ≥ 0 Q ≤ 0 Wout = Q ≤ 0

Therefore no cyclic device can have as its sole effect extracting heat from one reservoir and delivering positive work. This is the Kelvin–Planck statement. For example, taking 100 J from a 300 K reservoir and producing only work would give ΔStotal = −100/300 ≈ −0.333 J/K. The prohibited feature is the negative total entropy change, not an energy deficit.

Could an irreversible engine avoid the contradiction by producing entropy inside itself?

No. If it completes a cycle its total entropy change is still zero. Internal entropy generation would have to be balanced by entropy exported elsewhere. The proposed sole-effect arrangement provides no compensating entropy increase.

4. Why a single expansion is allowed

An ideal gas can absorb heat from one bath and convert all of it into work during a reversible isothermal expansion. For fixed gas amount n and V₂ > V₁,

ΔUgas = 0 Qin = Wout = nRT ln(V₂/V₁) > 0 ΔSgas = nR ln(V₂/V₁) = Qin/T ΔSbath = −Qin/T ΔStotal = 0

The gas finishes expanded and has gained entropy, so this step is not a complete engine cycle. Reversing this particular path at the same bath temperature requires work input nRT ln(V₂/V₁), cancelling the work gained. That example illustrates the role of resetting the device; the preceding entropy proof establishes the general cyclic prohibition.

5. Keep the logical starting point explicit

There are two useful routes. In the reversible-heat lesson, take Kelvin–Planck as the empirical starting statement, prove Carnot’s theorem, establish the reversible cycle integral, and define entropy. On this page, assume entropy and ΔStotal ≥ 0 are already known and deduce Kelvin–Planck. Do not use this alternative entropy proof as a premise inside the earlier construction of entropy; that would be circular.

A temperature difference, chemical fuel, a pressure difference, or consumption of a prepared state supplies additional resources or effects. Such arrangements do not meet the single-reservoir, cyclic, sole-effect conditions used here.
