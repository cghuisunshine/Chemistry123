Derive the internal-energy volume identity

Where did that derivative identity come from?

Use the Helmholtz free energy

Define A = U − TS for a fixed-composition simple compressible equilibrium system. Then dA = −S dT − P dV. Equality of mixed partial derivatives of the smooth state function A(T,V) gives a Maxwell relation.

(∂S/∂V)T = (∂P/∂T)V

Differentiate U while holding T fixed

Along a constant-temperature variation, the fundamental relation dU = T dS − P dV has coefficient T(∂S/∂V)T − P in front of dV. Substitution proves the identity.

(∂U/∂V)T = T(∂S/∂V)T − P = T(∂P/∂T)V − P

Test a nonideal model

For the molar van der Waals equation P = RT/(v − b) − a/v² with constant a and b, the identity gives (∂u/∂v)T = a/v². Integration at fixed T yields u(T,v) = f(T) − a/v. Expansion reduces the magnitude of the attractive potential energy and raises u at fixed T.

The ideal-gas limit a = b = 0 returns zero volume dependence. This is a consistency calculation within that equation-of-state model.
