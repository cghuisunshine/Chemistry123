Why Cₚ − Cᵥ equals nR

Show why an ideal gas needs more heat per degree at constant pressure than at constant volume.

The equation and what it means

Cₚ − Cᵥ = nR Molar form: Cₚ,ₘ − Cᵥ,ₘ = R

Show why an ideal gas needs more heat per degree at constant pressure than at constant volume.

Symbols and units. Cₚ, Cᵥ: sample heat capacities (J K⁻¹); Cₚ,ₘ, Cᵥ,ₘ: molar heat capacities (J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹); n: mol.

What can we start from?

  • Definition. H = U + PV.
  • Model. For an ideal gas, PV = nRT and U depends only on T at fixed amount.
  • Definition. Cᵥ = (∂U/∂T)ᵥ and Cₚ = (∂H/∂T)ₚ for a fixed amount.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Rewrite H using the gas model

Replace PV in the enthalpy definition.

H(T) = U(T) + nRT

For this model, both U and H depend only on temperature for fixed n and composition.

↳ Follow-up: Can PV = nRT justify this extra premise? Why does an ideal gas have U = U(T)?Follow the argument, then return to this exact statement. ↗

2. Differentiate with respect to T

Hold n fixed and differentiate the entire equation.

dH/dT = dU/dT + nR

Because U and H depend only on T, these derivatives are the relevant constant-volume and constant-pressure heat capacities.

3. Identify the heat capacities

Replace derivatives by Cₚ and Cᵥ; then divide by n for the molar result.

Cₚ = Cᵥ + nR Cₚ,ₘ − Cᵥ,ₘ = R

At constant pressure the gas expands as it warms. Extra heat supplies that expansion work.

Conditions and limits

  • The amount and chemical composition of the gas are fixed.
  • The gas obeys the ideal-gas model, including U = U(T).

Capital C here is the heat capacity of the whole sample (J K⁻¹). The difference is R only for molar heat capacities.

Common misconception

The gas must be monatomic.

The heat capacities depend on molecular degrees of freedom, but their difference is nR for any fixed-composition ideal gas.

Check the result

For a monatomic ideal gas, Cᵥ,ₘ = 3R/2 and Cₚ,ₘ = 5R/2. Their difference is R.

For 2.00 mol of ideal gas, what is Cₚ − Cᵥ for the entire sample?

The sample difference is nR = 2.00 × 8.314 = 16.63 J K⁻¹. The molar difference is R.
