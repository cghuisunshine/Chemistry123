What replaces Cₚ − Cᵥ = nR for a real fluid?

How special is the ideal-gas result?

Compare two entropy derivatives

For a fixed sample, Cₚ = T(∂S/∂T)P and Cᵥ = T(∂S/∂T)V. Apply the chain rule to S(T,V) along a constant-pressure path, then use (∂S/∂V)T = (∂P/∂T)V.

Cₚ − Cᵥ = T(∂P/∂T)V(∂V/∂T)P

Express it using measurable responses

Define the thermal expansion coefficient α = (1/V)(∂V/∂T)P and the isothermal compressibility κT = −(1/V)(∂V/∂P)T. Setting dV = 0 in the equation for V(T,P) gives (∂P/∂T)V = α/κT.

Cₚ − Cᵥ = TVα²/κT

For a mechanically stable phase κT is positive, so Cₚ ≥ Cᵥ. The dimensions are K·m³·K⁻²·Pa = J K⁻¹.

Recover the ideal limit

ideal gas: α = 1/T, κT = 1/P TVα²/κT = PV/T = nR

The difference is therefore independent of the number of active internal molecular modes. Both heat capacities may vary with T while their ideal-gas difference remains nR.
