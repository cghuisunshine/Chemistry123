What does the wavefunction actually measure?

Why must its normalization and dimensions be specified?

Interpret a probability density

∫ |ψ(x)|² dx = 1 Prob(a ≤ x ≤ b) = ∫ab |ψ(x)|² dx

In one dimension |ψ|² has units inverse length, so ψ has units length to the power −1/2. A value of |ψ|² at one point is a density, not itself a finite probability. The dimensions change with the dimensionality and coordinate measure of the problem.

Distinguish normalization models

A plane wave has constant |ψ|² and cannot be normalized by an ordinary integral over the whole infinite line. On a specified periodic interval of length L, the constant magnitude can instead be 1/√L, with allowed k = 2πn/L. A localized free particle can be represented by a normalizable wave packet. These boundary models describe different physical situations.
