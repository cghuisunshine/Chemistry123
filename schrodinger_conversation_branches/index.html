The Schrödinger energy equation

State the quantum postulate, construct its energy operator, and verify a simple energy eigenfunction.

The equation and what it means

−(ℏ²/2m)d²ψ/dx² + V(x)ψ = Eψ

State the quantum postulate, construct its energy operator, and verify a simple energy eigenfunction.

Symbols and units. ψ: wavefunction (units m⁻¹ᐟ² when normalized over a one-dimensional position coordinate); ℏ = h/(2π): J s; m: kg; x: m; V and E: J. |ψ|²dx is a probability.

Textbook source. Equation 8.13. Open the exact expression and its context.

What can we start from?

  • Postulate. A nonrelativistic particle is described by a wavefunction; stationary states obey Ĥψ = Eψ. This is a quantum premise, not a result derived from classical conservation alone.
  • Operator model. In one dimension, p̂ = −iℏ d/dx and Ĥ = p̂²/(2m) + V(x).
  • Calculus. d²exp(ikx)/dx² = −k²exp(ikx).
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Construct the kinetic-energy operator

Apply the momentum operator twice.

p̂²ψ = −ℏ² d²ψ/dx² Ĥψ = −(ℏ²/2m)d²ψ/dx² + V(x)ψ

The minus sign follows from applying the imaginary momentum operator twice.

2. Write the stationary eigenvalue condition

Use the stated energy-eigenstate postulate.

−(ℏ²/2m)d²ψ/dx² + V(x)ψ = Eψ

An energy eigenfunction retains its spatial probability distribution during stationary evolution.

3. Check a free-particle solution

Set V = 0 and insert ψ = C exp(ikx).

E = ℏ²k²/(2m)

Energy is nonnegative. On a periodic interval of length L, normalization gives |C| = 1/√L and k = 2πn/L; this is a stated boundary model.

Conditions and limits

  • The model is one nonrelativistic particle in one dimension with a time-independent scalar potential.
  • Boundary conditions and normalization must be imposed to identify physical states and allowed energies.

Constructing the differential equation from the stated quantum operators is not a derivation of quantum mechanics from classical physics. The time-dependent equation is a separate dynamical postulate.

Common misconception

Every mathematical solution is automatically a normalizable bound state.

Boundary conditions and normalization matter. A free-particle plane wave on the infinite line is not an ordinary square-integrable bound state.

Check the result

For a particle confined by infinite walls at x = 0 and L, ψₙ = √(2/L) sin(nπx/L) and Eₙ = n²π²ℏ²/(2mL²), n = 1,2,…; both wall boundary conditions are satisfied.

For a free particle with E = ℏ²k²/(2m), what happens if |k| doubles?

Energy is proportional to k². Positive and negative k of the same magnitude have the same free-particle energy.
