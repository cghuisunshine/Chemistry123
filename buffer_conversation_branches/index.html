Henderson–Hasselbalch equation

Derive the buffer equation from acid equilibrium, then identify the approximation behind using mixing concentrations.

The equation and what it means

pH = pKₐ + log₁₀(a_A⁻/a_HA) Dilute approximation: pH ≈ pKₐ + log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA])

Derive the buffer equation from acid equilibrium, then identify the approximation behind using mixing concentrations.

Symbols and units. a: dimensionless activity; Kₐ: dimensionless acid equilibrium constant; pH and pKₐ: dimensionless; [A⁻], [HA]: equilibrium concentrations in matching units.

What can we start from?

  • Equilibrium. For HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻, Kₐ = a_H⁺ a_A⁻ / a_HA (water treated as solvent).
  • Definition. pH = −log₁₀ a_H⁺ and pKₐ = −log₁₀ Kₐ.
  • Log rule. log(x/y) = log x − log y.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Isolate hydrogen-ion activity

Rearrange the acid equilibrium expression to solve for a_H⁺.

a_H⁺ = Kₐ a_HA/a_A⁻

Increasing conjugate base relative to acid lowers hydrogen-ion activity.

2. Take negative base-10 logarithms

Apply −log₁₀ to both sides and expand the product and ratio.

−log₁₀ a_H⁺ = −log₁₀ Kₐ − log₁₀(a_HA/a_A⁻)

The acid/base ratio will invert when its logarithm changes sign.

3. Introduce pH and pKₐ

Replace the definitions and reverse the ratio.

pH = pKₐ + log₁₀(a_A⁻/a_HA)

Equal acid and conjugate-base activities give pH = pKₐ.

4. State the concentration approximation

When can you write the ratio as [A⁻]/[HA], and what extra care is needed with initial mixing amounts?

pH ≈ pKₐ + log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA])

Use equilibrium concentrations. For a prepared buffer, first account for any stoichiometric reaction with added strong acid or base.

Conditions and limits

  • HA and its conjugate base A⁻ are at equilibrium.
  • To replace the activity ratio by a concentration ratio, activity-coefficient effects are negligible or cancel.

The activity form is an algebraic rearrangement of Kₐ. The concentration form and the use of prepared amounts introduce separate approximations.

Common misconception

Initial mixing amounts can always replace equilibrium concentrations, even after adding a large amount of strong acid.

Use equilibrium concentrations. Initial amounts approximate them only after neutralization bookkeeping and when subsequent dissociation changes are small.

Check the result

If pKₐ = 4.76 and [A⁻]/[HA] = 2.00, pH ≈ 4.76 + 0.301 = 5.06.

A dilute equilibrium buffer has [A⁻]/[HA] = 10. What is its pH?

log₁₀ 10 = 1, so pH is one unit above pKₐ. More conjugate base gives a higher pH.
