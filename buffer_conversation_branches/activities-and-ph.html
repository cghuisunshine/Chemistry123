Why use activities in the buffer equation?

Is pH simply minus log concentration?

Start with a thermodynamic equilibrium constant

For HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻, take solvent water into the acid standard-state convention. At equilibrium the thermodynamic constant is Kₐ = aH⁺aA⁻/aHA. Every activity is dimensionless.

pH = −log₁₀ aH⁺ pH = pKₐ + log₁₀(aA⁻/aHA)

Expose the concentration approximation

aᵢ = γᵢcᵢ/c° pH = pKₐ + log₁₀(cA⁻/cHA) + log₁₀(γA⁻/γHA)

The standard concentration cancels in the acid/base ratio. The activity-coefficient ratio must be close to 1 before the usual concentration formula follows. Ionic strength can make this correction significant.

Keep two approximations distinct

Approximating activities by equilibrium concentrations is one step. Replacing equilibrium concentrations by prepared amounts is a second step. Neither follows merely from taking logarithms. A buffer can be concentrated enough to minimize dissociation changes yet nonideal enough to require activity corrections.
