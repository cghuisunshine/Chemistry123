Derive the buffer capacity

Why does an equal acid/base mixture resist pH change best?

Use an ideal dilute monoprotic model

Let C = [HA] + [A⁻] be the fixed analytical buffer concentration, h = [H⁺], and κ = Kₐc°. Neglect dilution during titration and hold activity coefficients constant. Acid equilibrium gives the deprotonated fraction f = κ/(κ + h).

Differentiate the amount of bound base

A small amount of strong base raises the deprotonated concentration Cf. Since dh/dpH = −(ln 10)h in this ideal model, its buffer contribution per pH unit is:

βbuffer = C df/dpH = (ln 10) Cκh/(κ + h)²

β has concentration units per pH unit. The factor κh/(κ + h)² is largest at h = κ, corresponding to pH = pKₐ and equal HA and A⁻ concentrations.

Include the solvent when necessary

βtotal = (ln 10)[Cκh/(κ + h)² + h + Kw(c°)²/h]

The last terms represent free hydrogen and hydroxide ions. The buffer-only maximum is (ln 10)C/4. Doubling C doubles this contribution without changing the pH of an equal-ratio mixture, within the stated ideal model.
