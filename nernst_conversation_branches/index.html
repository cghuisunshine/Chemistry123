Start with the equation E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q

The Nernst equation

Connect the electrical work of a reversible cell to the composition dependence of reaction Gibbs energy.

The equation and what it means

E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q At 298.15 K: E = E° − (0.05916 V/n) log₁₀ Q

Connect the electrical work of a reversible cell to the composition dependence of reaction Gibbs energy.

Symbols and units. E, E°: V; n: moles of electrons per mole of reaction (stoichiometric number); F = 96485 C mol⁻¹; R: J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; T: K; 1 V = 1 J C⁻¹.

What can we start from?

  • Electrical. For one mole of reaction transferring n moles of electrons, the charge magnitude is nF; reversible electrical work on the system is −nFE.
  • Thermodynamics. At fixed T,P, reversible non-PV work on a closed system equals its Gibbs change, giving ΔᵣG = −nFE.
  • Reaction. ΔᵣG = ΔᵣG° + RT ln Q; consistently, ΔᵣG° = −nFE°.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Substitute the electrical relations

Replace ΔᵣG and ΔᵣG° in the reaction Gibbs relation.

−nFE = −nFE° + RT ln Q

Chemical free energy and electrical potential describe the same reversible driving force.

2. Solve for the cell potential

Divide every term by −nF, keeping the sign of the logarithmic term.

E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q

As Q increases at fixed T, the potential for the written reaction decreases.

↳ Follow-up: Which concentrations belong in a cell equation? How do I construct Q and handle the signs?Follow the argument, then return to this exact statement. ↗

3. Convert to base-10 if useful

Use ln Q = (ln 10) log₁₀ Q and evaluate the coefficient at 298.15 K.

E = E° − (2.302585 RT/nF) log₁₀ Q At 298.15 K: 2.302585 RT/F = 0.05916 V

The familiar decimal coefficient has temperature and voltage units built into it.

4. Recover the equilibrium connection

Set E = 0 and Q = K. Solve for E°.

E° = (RT/nF) ln K

A positive E° corresponds to K > 1 for the written cell reaction, consistent with ΔᵣG° = −nFE°.

Conditions and limits

  • E is the reversible cell potential at the specified T, not a loaded voltage with irreversible losses.
  • The balanced cell reaction, electron number n, and dimensionless activity quotient Q use the same reaction direction.

A pure solid or liquid in its standard phase has activity approximately 1. At cell equilibrium E = 0 and Q = K, while E° can be nonzero.

Common misconception

The cell must be at 25 °C for the general Nernst equation.

The general equation uses RT/nF at any applicable temperature. Only the 0.05916 V coefficient assumes 298.15 K.

Check the result

If E° = 1.10 V, n = 2, and Q = 100 at 298.15 K, the reversible potential is about 1.041 V.

At 298.15 K, n = 2 and Q = 100. What is E − E°?

E − E° = −(0.05916/2) log₁₀ 100 = −0.05916 V.
