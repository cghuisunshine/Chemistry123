Why does reversibility maximize electrical output?

Where does dissipated free energy go?

Keep entropy production in the energy balance

For a closed cell at fixed T and P exchanging heat with a reservoir at T, include electrical work on the cell. Energy and entropy balances give:

ΔG = welectrical,on − T Sgen welectrical,out = −ΔG − T Sgen

Assume the stated thermal and mechanical surroundings account for all entropy changes. The entropy generated is nonnegative.

Identify the lost useful work

lost electrical work = T Sgen ≥ 0

In the reversible limit Sgen = 0 and all of −ΔG can become electrical work. At finite current, some available work is dissipated and eventually appears as additional heat in the surroundings. Energy is conserved even when useful work is lost.

Apply the statement locally during discharge

As composition changes, E and ΔᵣG both change. Reversible work over a finite discharge is ∫nFE(ξ)dξ = −∫ΔᵣG(ξ)dξ, not generally one initial voltage times the whole charge. The Nernst equation supplies the local reversible potential at each composition.
