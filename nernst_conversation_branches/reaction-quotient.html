How do I construct Q and handle the signs?

Which concentrations belong in a cell equation?

Balance the written cell reaction first

For Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s), n = 2. Pure solid activities are approximately 1 in their usual standard states, so the activity quotient and reversible potential are:

Q = aZn²⁺/aCu²⁺ E = E° − (RT/2F) ln Q

At 298.15 K, Q = 100 lowers E by 0.05916 V. The denominator is 2 because the balanced reaction transfers two electrons.

Reversing and scaling are different operations

Reversing the reaction sends Q to 1/Q, ΔᵣG° to −ΔᵣG°, and E° to −E°. The electron-transfer magnitude n stays positive. Multiplying the entire reaction by λ sends Q to Qλ and n to λn, leaving E unchanged.

(RT/(λnF)) ln(Qλ) = (RT/nF) ln Q

Voltage is intensive. Reaction energy and transferred charge scale together.

At equilibrium the actual voltage vanishes

Q = K, E = 0 E° = (RT/nF) ln K

A nonzero E° is compatible with E = 0 because equilibrium generally occurs at Q different from 1. The 0.05916 V decimal-log coefficient is specific to 298.15 K.
