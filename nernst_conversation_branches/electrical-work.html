Why is ΔᵣG = −nFE?

How does cell voltage measure chemical free energy?

Convert reaction extent into charge

Let the balanced cell reaction transfer n electrons per formula reaction, with extent ξ measured in moles of reaction. The magnitude of charge delivered through the external circuit is dqcharge = nF dξ. Here n is a stoichiometric number, not the amount of gas used in PV = nRT.

δwelectrical,out = E dqcharge = nFE dξ

Use the reversible useful-work theorem

At fixed T and P, the maximum non-PV work output is −dG. A reversible cell reaches that limit; electrical work on the cell is the negative of its output.

dG = δwelectrical,on,rev = −nFE dξ ΔᵣG = (∂G/∂ξ)T,P = −nFE

The units are (mol electrons/mol reaction)(C/mol electrons)(J/C) = J/mol reaction. A positive reversible E corresponds to a negative forward reaction driving force.

A loaded voltage can be different

A finite current produces resistance losses, electrode overpotentials, and concentration gradients. The measured terminal voltage then differs from the reversible E used in the Nernst equation. Inferring equilibrium chemical free energy from a loaded voltage requires accounting for those irreversible effects.
