Start with the equation k = A exp(−Ea/RT)

The Arrhenius equation

Connect the empirical temperature dependence of a rate constant to a straight-line plot and a two-temperature prediction.

The equation and what it means

k = A exp(−Ea/RT) ln(k₂/k₁) = −(Ea/R)(1/T₂ − 1/T₁)

Connect the empirical temperature dependence of a rate constant to a straight-line plot and a two-temperature prediction.

Symbols and units. Ea: J mol⁻¹; T: K; R: J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; A has the same units as k (for example s⁻¹ for first order). k₂/k₁ and Ea/(RT) are dimensionless.

Textbook source. Equation 22.29. Open the exact expression and its context.

What can we start from?

  • Empirical model. Over the chosen interval, k(T) = A exp[−Ea/(RT)] with approximately constant A and activation energy Ea.
  • Logarithms. ln(exp x) = x and ln(u/v) = ln u − ln v.
  • Plot convention. A straight line y = b + mx has slope m and intercept b; compare rate constants in one fixed unit.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Take a logarithm

Apply ln to the empirical exponential using a common reference unit kref.

ln(k/kref) = ln(A/kref) − Ea/(RT)

The exponential penalty decreases as temperature rises when Ea > 0.

2. Identify the plot slope

Choose horizontal coordinate 1/T.

slope of ln(k/kref) versus 1/T = −Ea/R Ea = −R × slope

The slope has temperature units; multiplying by R gives molar energy.

3. Eliminate the prefactor

Write the equation at two temperatures and subtract.

ln(k₂/k₁) = −(Ea/R)(1/T₂ − 1/T₁)

This predicts the change in rate constant without separately knowing A.

Conditions and limits

  • One Arrhenius model describes the same reaction mechanism and rate-constant definition over the temperature interval.
  • A and Ea are approximately constant for the straight-line and two-temperature expressions.

Arrhenius behaviour is an empirical model, not a universal consequence of a balanced reaction. Mechanism changes and temperature-dependent prefactors can curve an Arrhenius plot. For strict dimensionless logs, use ln(k/kref) and ln(A/kref) in a fixed rate-constant unit.

Common misconception

Activation energy must equal the reaction enthalpy.

Activation energy characterizes rate-constant temperature dependence, while reaction enthalpy compares products with reactants. They are different quantities.

Check the result

With Ea = 50.0 kJ mol⁻¹ from 300 K to 310 K, ln(k₂/k₁) ≈ 0.647, so k₂/k₁ ≈ 1.91.

An Arrhenius plot has slope −6000 K. Approximately what is Ea?

Ea = −R × (−6000 K) = 49,884 J mol⁻¹, approximately 49.9 kJ mol⁻¹.
