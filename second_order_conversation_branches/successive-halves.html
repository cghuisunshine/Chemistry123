Why do successive half-lives get longer?

Can we see the slowing directly?

Solve explicitly for concentration

c(t) = c₀/(1 + kc₀t)

At the first half-life τ = 1/(kc₀), concentration is c₀/2. To reach c₀/4, the total elapsed time is 3τ. The second halving interval is therefore 2τ, not τ.

Generalize to repeated halvings

c(tₘ) = c₀/2m tₘ = (2m − 1)/(kc₀) tₘ − tₘ₋₁ = 2m−1/(kc₀)

Each new halving begins at half the previous concentration, so the next interval doubles. The decreasing encounter frequency is reflected in the algebra.

Compare fractional disappearance rates

−(1/c) dc/dt = kc (second order) −(1/c) dc/dt = k (first order)

For second order, even the fraction disappearing per second falls as c falls. With k = 0.50 L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹ and c₀ = 0.20 mol L⁻¹, the first three halving intervals are 10 s, 20 s, and 40 s.
