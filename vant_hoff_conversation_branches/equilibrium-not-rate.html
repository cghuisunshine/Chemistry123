Why does heating change K without specifying the rate?

Does a larger K mean a faster reaction?

Equilibrium is an endpoint statement

d ln K/dT = ΔᵣH°/(RT²)

An endothermic written reaction has ΔᵣH° > 0, so K increases with T. This changes the equilibrium activity ratio. It does not state how quickly the mixture approaches that ratio.

A simple kinetic model makes the distinction visible

For an elementary reversible first-order ideal-dilute model A ⇌ B, equilibrium requires kf[A] = kr[B], so K = kf/kr with compatible activity conventions. Both rate constants may increase with temperature, yet their ratio can either increase or decrease.

ln K = ln kf − ln kr

A catalyst can increase both rates while preserving their equilibrium ratio. It changes the time scale, not the thermodynamic equilibrium constant.

Check the sign numerically

For approximately constant ΔᵣH° = +40.0 kJ mol⁻¹ between 300 K and 310 K, ln(K₂/K₁) = (40000/8.314)(1/300 − 1/310) ≈ 0.517. Hence K₂/K₁ ≈ 1.68. No activation energy was needed for this equilibrium prediction.
