Derive the Gibbs–Helmholtz relation

Why differentiate G°/T rather than G°?

Use the natural differential of G

For fixed composition dG = −S dT + V dP. At fixed standard pressure the temperature derivative of each standard chemical potential is minus its standard molar entropy. Summing with stoichiometric coefficients gives dΔᵣG°/dT = −ΔᵣS°.

d(ΔᵣG°/T)/dT = [T(dΔᵣG°/dT) − ΔᵣG°]/T²

The entropy contribution cancels

T(dΔᵣG°/dT) − ΔᵣG° = −TΔᵣS° − (ΔᵣH° − TΔᵣS°) = −ΔᵣH°

Therefore d(ΔᵣG°/T)/dT = −ΔᵣH°/T². Differentiating G°/T is useful because equilibrium gives ΔᵣG°/T = −R ln K directly.

No constant-enthalpy assumption yet

Every quantity in the cancellation is evaluated at the same temperature. ΔᵣH° and ΔᵣS° may both vary with T. Constant enthalpy is required only when replacing the later temperature integral by a simple two-temperature formula.

This distinction prevents treating a narrow-range approximation as a general thermodynamic law.
