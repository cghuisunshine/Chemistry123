How do heat capacities correct van ’t Hoff?

What if ΔᵣH° changes over the temperature range?

Use Kirchhoff’s relation

For the same reaction and standard phases at fixed standard pressure, define ΔᵣCₚ° = ΣνᵢCₚ,ₘ,ᵢ°. The temperature derivative of reaction enthalpy is this reaction heat-capacity difference.

dΔᵣH°/dT = ΔᵣCₚ° ΔᵣH°(T) = ΔᵣH°(T₁) + ΔᵣCₚ°(T − T₁)

The second line assumes ΔᵣCₚ° is constant over the interval, a less restrictive approximation than constant ΔᵣH°.

Integrate the differential equation

ln(K₂/K₁) = [ΔᵣH°(T₁)/R](1/T₁ − 1/T₂) + (ΔᵣCₚ°/R)[ln(T₂/T₁) + T₁/T₂ − 1]

This follows by integrating [ΔᵣH°(T₁) + ΔᵣCₚ°(T − T₁)]/(RT²). Setting ΔᵣCₚ° = 0 recovers the usual formula. The bracket multiplying ΔᵣCₚ° vanishes at T₂ = T₁.

Watch for a change of physical model

A phase transition in a standard-state species can introduce a discontinuity in entropy or enthalpy derivatives. Integrate piecewise with the appropriate phases and transition data. Extrapolating one constant heat-capacity model through such a transition is not justified.
