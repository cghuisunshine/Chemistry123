Why are phase chemical potentials equal?

What prevents a molecule from preferring one phase?

Transfer a small amount

At fixed temperature and pressure, transfer dn moles of a pure substance from phase α to phase β while conserving the total amount. The Gibbs change is the difference in chemical potentials times dn.

dG = (μβ − μα) dn

If this difference were negative, transfer to β would lower G. If positive, the reverse transfer would lower G. At interior equilibrium neither direction is favoured.

Move to a neighbouring coexistence state

μα = μβ dμα = dμβ

The second equality describes movement along the coexistence curve. At an arbitrary displacement away from that curve, the two chemical potentials need not stay equal. This is why the resulting derivative is the slope of a phase boundary.
