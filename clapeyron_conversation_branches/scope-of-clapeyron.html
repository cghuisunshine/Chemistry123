Where does the exact equation stop applying?

Can we divide by a zero transition volume?

Use a pure-substance boundary

The compact differential dμ = Vₘ dp − Sₘ dT applies to each pure phase. In a multicomponent mixture the chemical potentials depend on composition as well, so additional terms enter when composition changes. A phase diagram for a mixture cannot generally be described by this pure-substance slope alone.

Handle limiting points carefully

The derivative form divides by ΔVₘ. At a liquid–vapour critical point the phases become identical and both ΔHₘ and ΔVₘ tend to zero. Substitution gives an indeterminate ratio, not an infinite or zero slope by itself. A limiting equation of state or appropriate response data are needed.
