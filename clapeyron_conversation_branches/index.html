The Clapeyron equation

Explain how entropy and volume changes set the slope of a pure substance’s phase boundary.

The equation and what it means

dp/dT = ΔₜᵣₛSₘ/ΔₜᵣₛVₘ = ΔₜᵣₛHₘ/(TΔₜᵣₛVₘ)

Explain how entropy and volume changes set the slope of a pure substance’s phase boundary.

Symbols and units. p: Pa; T: K; Sₘ: J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; Vₘ: m³ mol⁻¹; Hₘ: J mol⁻¹. The slope has units Pa K⁻¹.

Textbook source. Equation 4.6. Open the exact expression and its context.

What can we start from?

  • Equilibrium. Along a pure-substance phase boundary, μᵅ(T,p) = μᵝ(T,p).
  • Fundamental. For either pure phase, dμ = Vₘ dp − Sₘ dT.
  • Transition. At coexistence, ΔₜᵣₛGₘ = 0, so ΔₜᵣₛHₘ = TΔₜᵣₛSₘ.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Move along the equilibrium boundary

Differentiate the equality of the phase chemical potentials.

Vₘᵅ dp − Sₘᵅ dT = Vₘᵝ dp − Sₘᵝ dT

A small move along coexistence keeps the chemical potentials equal.

2. Collect transition differences

Gather the volume terms and entropy terms using β minus α.

ΔₜᵣₛVₘ dp = ΔₜᵣₛSₘ dT dp/dT = ΔₜᵣₛSₘ/ΔₜᵣₛVₘ

More entropy favours the high-temperature phase; pressure favours the smaller-volume phase.

3. Replace entropy at coexistence

Use zero transition Gibbs energy at equilibrium.

dp/dT = ΔₜᵣₛHₘ/(TΔₜᵣₛVₘ)

The slope can now be estimated from latent heat and the volume change.

Conditions and limits

  • The two phases of one pure substance remain in equilibrium along the boundary.
  • Transition quantities are evaluated at the same coexistence temperature and pressure; ΔₜᵣₛVₘ is nonzero.

Define every transition change as phase β minus phase α. At a critical point, both numerator and denominator can vanish; evaluate the limiting behaviour rather than dividing two zeros.

Common misconception

The vapour must be ideal.

The exact Clapeyron equation requires no ideal-gas approximation. That approximation enters the Clausius–Clapeyron reduction.

Check the result

If ΔHₘ = 6000 J mol⁻¹, T = 273 K and ΔVₘ = −1.6 × 10⁻⁶ m³ mol⁻¹, dp/dT ≈ −1.37 × 10⁷ Pa K⁻¹.

Melting absorbs heat but decreases molar volume, as for ice near ordinary pressure. What is the sign of dp/dT?

ΔfusHₘ > 0 and ΔfusVₘ < 0 give a negative slope. Increasing pressure lowers the melting temperature in this regime.
