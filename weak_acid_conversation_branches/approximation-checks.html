How small must dissociation be?

Does a small Kₐ guarantee the square-root formula?

Use a dimensionless expansion parameter

Set y = x/c₀ and ε = κ/c₀. The quadratic becomes y² = ε(1 − y). Small dissociation means y ≪ 1, so y ≈ √ε. It depends on κ/c₀, not on Kₐ alone.

x ≈ √(κc₀) x/c₀ ≈ √(κ/c₀)

Quantify the error rather than naming a rule

If the true dissociation fraction is y, the square-root estimate divided by the quadratic value is 1/√(1 − y). At y = 0.05 the concentration overestimate is about 2.60%. The common 5% dissociation rule is therefore a tolerance choice, not a thermodynamic threshold.

xapprox/xquadratic = (1 − y)−1/2

Check water independently

Require the predicted hydrogen concentration to be much greater than √Kwc°. A weak acid can be so dilute that water controls the pH even if a small-dissociation check appears harmless. For Kₐ = 10⁻⁵, c₀ = 0.100 mol L⁻¹, and c° = 1 mol L⁻¹, the square-root estimate is 0.00100 mol L⁻¹, whereas the quadratic gives about 0.000995 mol L⁻¹.
