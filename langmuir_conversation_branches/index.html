The Langmuir adsorption isotherm

Balance adsorption onto empty sites against desorption from occupied sites to predict fractional coverage.

The equation and what it means

θ = Kp/(1 + Kp), with K = ka/kd

Balance adsorption onto empty sites against desorption from occupied sites to predict fractional coverage.

Symbols and units. θ: dimensionless occupied fraction; p: gas pressure (for example Pa); ka: Pa⁻¹ s⁻¹ and kd: s⁻¹ for this per-site rate convention; K = ka/kd: Pa⁻¹.

Textbook source. Equation 25.4. Open the exact expression and its context.

What can we start from?

  • Site model. θ is the occupied fraction of equivalent independent sites, with 0 ≤ θ ≤ 1 and only one adsorbate per site.
  • Rate model. Per-site adsorption rate is ka p(1 − θ); desorption rate is kd θ.
  • Equilibrium. At fixed temperature, equilibrium coverage is stationary because adsorption and desorption rates are equal.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Balance the competing rates

Set adsorption and desorption rates equal.

ka p(1 − θ) = kd θ

Microscopic exchange continues at equilibrium even though the total occupied fraction is stationary.

2. Collect the coverage terms

Expand the left side and gather θ.

ka p = (kd + ka p)θ θ = ka p/(kd + ka p)

The denominator accounts for both adsorption and desorption tendencies.

3. Introduce K and test the limits

Divide numerator and denominator by kd.

θ = Kp/(1 + Kp) Kp ≪ 1: θ ≈ Kp; Kp ≫ 1: θ → 1

At low pressure coverage rises almost linearly; saturation limits it to one monolayer.

Conditions and limits

  • Adsorption forms a monolayer on equivalent sites without interactions between adsorbed molecules.
  • Adsorption is nondissociative and the stated rates describe equilibrium at fixed temperature.

Dissociative adsorption, multiple site types, lateral interactions, and multilayer adsorption require different models. With pressure-based rates, K has inverse-pressure units; Kp is dimensionless.

Common misconception

Every collision with a fully covered surface creates a second layer.

The Langmuir model allows only monolayer occupancy. The empty-site factor tends to zero as coverage approaches one.

Check the result

For K = 0.020 kPa⁻¹ and p = 50 kPa, Kp = 1 and θ = 0.50. At 100 kPa, θ = 2/3.

At what pressure does the model predict half coverage?

Set θ = 1/2. Then 1 + Kp = 2Kp, so Kp = 1 and p = 1/K.
