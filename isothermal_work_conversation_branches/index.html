Reversible isothermal work

Derive the logarithm in the reversible expansion work of an ideal gas.

The equation and what it means

wrev = −nRT ln(V₂/V₁)

Derive the logarithm in the reversible expansion work of an ideal gas.

Symbols and units. n: mol; R: J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; T: K; V₂/V₁: dimensionless; w_rev: J.

Textbook source. Equation 2.9 · Equation 2.10 · Equation 2.11. Open the exact expression and its context.

What can we start from?

  • Boundary work. δw = −P_ext dV.
  • Model. PV = nRT for an ideal gas.
  • Calculus. ∫ dV/V = ln V + constant; use a dimensionless ratio in the final result.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Use reversibility

What does mechanical reversibility allow you to substitute for P_ext in the work integral?

wrev = −∫V₁V₂ P dV

Use the system pressure only because the path is mechanically reversible.

2. Insert the equation of state

Express P in terms of V and identify which factors are constant.

wrev = −nRT ∫V₁V₂ dV/V

The pressure decreases during expansion, so −PΔV with one fixed system pressure would be wrong.

3. Integrate and interpret the sign

Evaluate the integral. Is work on the gas positive or negative for expansion?

wrev = −nRT ln(V₂/V₁)

Expansion gives V₂/V₁ > 1, a positive logarithm, and negative work on the system.

4. Connect heat to work

For an ideal gas at fixed T, what is ΔU? Use the first law to find q_rev.

ΔU = 0 qrev = −wrev = nRT ln(V₂/V₁)

Heat entering the gas replaces the energy transferred out through work.

Conditions and limits

  • The gas is ideal, with fixed n and constant T.
  • The path is mechanically reversible, so external pressure tracks gas pressure in the reversible limit.

Free expansion and expansion against a fixed load have different work. This formula describes a specified reversible isothermal path.

Common misconception

External pressure stays constant while volume changes.

At constant T, ideal-gas pressure varies as 1/V along the reversible path.

Check the result

Reversing the path changes ln(V₂/V₁) to its negative, so reversible compression requires positive work on the gas.

One mole expands reversibly at 300 K from V to 2V. Approximately what is w?

w = −1 × 8.314 × 300 × ln 2 = −1729 J.
