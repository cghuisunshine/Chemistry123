How can temperature stay fixed while work leaves?

Where does the expansion energy come from?

Internal energy and heat are different concepts

For a fixed-composition ideal gas, U depends only on T. An isothermal process therefore has ΔU = 0, even though substantial heat and work can cross the boundary. Energy conservation constrains their sum.

0 = ΔU = q + w qrev = nRT ln(V₂/V₁)

The thermal reservoir supplies the work output

During a reversible expansion the moving piston removes energy. Heat from a reservoir at an infinitesimally higher temperature restores that energy without a finite temperature change of the gas. Strict reversibility is a limit in which the temperature difference and pressure imbalance both tend to zero.

Isothermal does not mean insulated. Insulation imposes q = 0; a reversible insulated expansion of an ideal gas normally cools it.

An insulated free expansion between the same ideal-gas endpoints has q = w = 0. Equal endpoint temperatures do not determine either transfer separately.
