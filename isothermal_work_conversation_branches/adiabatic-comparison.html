What if the gas is insulated instead?

Why does a reversible adiabatic expansion cool?

Combine the first law with q = 0

Assume fixed amount and composition, an ideal gas, reversible PV work, and a temperature-independent sample Cᵥ. Then dU = Cᵥ dT and the first law becomes:

Cᵥ dT = −P dV = −nRT dV/V

Integrate the separated variables

dT/T = −(nR/Cᵥ) dV/V T₂/T₁ = (V₁/V₂)nR/Cᵥ

Let γ = Cₚ/Cᵥ. Since Cₚ − Cᵥ = nR, the exponent nR/Cᵥ equals γ − 1. Combining this result with PV = nRT gives PVγ = constant along the reversible adiabat.

Check the sign and assumptions

Expansion makes V₂ > V₁, so T₂ < T₁. Work comes from internal energy because no heat enters. With variable Cᵥ, retain ∫ Cᵥ(T)dT/T rather than using a constant γ.

Insulation alone does not establish PVγ = constant. An insulated free expansion is irreversible, does zero PV work, and leaves an ideal gas at unchanged temperature.
