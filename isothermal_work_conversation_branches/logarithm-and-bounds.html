Why a logarithm, and why the reversible value?

What does the pressure–volume area tell us?

Equal ratios give equal contributions

On this ideal-gas isotherm, P = nRT/V. Integrating pressure over a small volume interval gives nRT dV/V. The fractional volume change matters: expansion from V to 2V gives the same work as expansion from 2V to 4V.

∫V₁V₂ dV/V = ln(V₂/V₁)

Writing ln V alone is shorthand for an antiderivative with a reference volume. The definite logarithmic ratio is dimensionless and independent of the chosen volume unit.

Compare with one constant external load

Choose Pext = nRT/V₂ and let the gas equilibrate against that load while a bath maintains the endpoint temperature. The constant-load expansion delivers less work than the reversible path.

−wload = nRT(1 − V₁/V₂) −wrev = nRT ln(V₂/V₁)

For doubling, the coefficients are 0.500 and 0.693. A finite pressure imbalance gives less output.

Compression has the corresponding cost

A finite pressure excess is needed to drive irreversible compression. With the same thermal constraints, reversible compression requires the least work input. Always state whether work is being counted on the system or delivered by it before comparing signs.
