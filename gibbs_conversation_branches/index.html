Gibbs energy from the second law

Derive a system-only spontaneity test from the entropy change of the system plus its surroundings.

The equation and what it means

ΔG = ΔH − TΔSsys = −TΔSuniv At fixed T and P: spontaneous change has ΔG < 0

Derive a system-only spontaneity test from the entropy change of the system plus its surroundings.

Symbols and units. G, H: J (or consistently kJ); S: J K⁻¹; T: K > 0. Keep ΔH and TΔS in the same energy units.

What can we start from?

  • Second law. ΔS_univ = ΔS_sys + ΔS_surr ≥ 0 for an allowed process of the isolated composite.
  • Reservoir. For a thermal reservoir at T, ΔS_surr = q_surr/T and q_surr = −q_sys.
  • Definition. G = H − TS; at constant pressure with only PV work, q_sys = ΔH_sys.
Prerequisites

We assume basic university mathematics and physics: differentiation, integration, vectors, energy conservation, and elementary probability. The chemical definitions and thermodynamic premises are stated explicitly; an empirical rate law or model is not proved by rearranging it.

1. Express surroundings entropy

Combine reservoir entropy with heat conservation.

ΔSsurr = −qsys/T

Use the reservoir temperature. The actual system need not follow a reversible path.

2. Replace heat by a state-function change

Use constant pressure and only PV work, then form ΔS_univ.

ΔSuniv = ΔSsys − ΔHsys/T

The surroundings contribution is now expressed using system quantities.

3. Multiply by −T

Rearrange the entropy sum into an energy quantity. What happens to the inequality?

−TΔSuniv = ΔHsys − TΔSsys ≤ 0

An increase in total entropy corresponds to a decrease in this system energy combination.

4. Identify the Gibbs change

Use G = H − TS at constant T. State the spontaneity and equilibrium criteria.

ΔG = −TΔSuniv Spontaneous: ΔG < 0; equilibrium: ΔG = 0

A negative Gibbs change is the second-law test under the stated constraints, not an extra law.

Conditions and limits

  • The system has constant T and P and exchanges heat with a reservoir at that same T.
  • The closed system does only PV work; all surroundings entropy exchange is accounted for.

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS for a finite change requires constant T. If T changes, use ΔG = ΔH − Δ(TS). Non-PV work needs an additional work term.

Common misconception

The system entropy must increase.

The second law concerns system plus surroundings. A decrease in system entropy can be outweighed by an increase in surroundings entropy.

Check the result

An endothermic process can be spontaneous if TΔS_sys exceeds ΔH. Always check the stated temperature and constraints.

At 300 K, a process has ΔH = +6.0 kJ and ΔS_sys = +30 J K⁻¹. Is the direction spontaneous at fixed T and P with only PV work?

TΔS = 300 × 30 J = 9.0 kJ. ΔG = 6.0 − 9.0 = −3.0 kJ. ΔS_univ = 10 J K⁻¹.
