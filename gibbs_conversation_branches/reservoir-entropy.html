Why is surroundings entropy −q/T?

May we use q/T for an irreversible process?

Model the surroundings as a reservoir

An ideal thermal reservoir is so large that absorbing a finite amount of energy does not appreciably change its temperature. It remains arbitrarily close to equilibrium, so its entropy change can be evaluated from its own equilibrium states.

dSres = dUres/T ΔSres = qres/T = −qsys/T

The reservoir is taken to exchange heat only in this balance. A separate mechanical pressure reservoir receives PV work without an entropy-transfer term.

The irreversible system is a separate issue

A rapidly reacting system can generate entropy internally, so its entropy change is generally not qsys/T. The reservoir formula does not impose reversibility on that system. When the surroundings consist only of the ideal heat and pressure reservoirs, their contributions are fully accounted for in the Gibbs argument.

A finite bath changes the calculation

If a bath with constant heat capacity C cools from T₁ to T₂, its entropy change is C ln(T₂/T₁), while its heat change is C(T₂ − T₁). Dividing by one arbitrarily selected temperature would be wrong. The compact ΔG = −TΔSuniv proof requires a fixed common reservoir temperature.
