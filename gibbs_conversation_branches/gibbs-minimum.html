Why does equilibrium minimize G?

Is ΔG = 0 sufficient to prove stability?

Hold the environmental constraints fixed

Consider an infinitesimal change in a closed system coupled to fixed T and P reservoirs, with no imposed non-PV work. The second law gives dG = −T dSuniv ≤ 0. The system can relax only toward accessible states of lower G.

Separate stationarity from stability

dG = (∂G/∂ξ)T,P dξ = ΔᵣG dξ interior equilibrium: ΔᵣG = 0 local stability: (∂²G/∂ξ²)T,P > 0

At an interior composition both signs of a sufficiently small reaction extent are allowed. A nonzero first derivative would permit a decrease in G in one direction. A zero derivative can also describe a maximum, so curvature matters.

Respect the feasible composition range

At a boundary where a species is absent, one direction of reaction may be impossible. The minimum must then be tested with the allowed variations rather than assuming an interior stationary point. Multiple reactions require a minimum with respect to every independent feasible extent.

A global equilibrium is the lowest accessible G under the conservation constraints. A local minimum separated by a barrier can instead be metastable.
